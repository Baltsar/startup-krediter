Item 17 in full: SME rates are 75% for industrial research and 50% for experimental development, but only with effective collaboration or wide dissemination; otherwise they are 15 lower. Under de minimis the rates are 60% and 35%, with a 300k EUR cap over three years that the Innobooster grant counts towards. SMEs get 0% overhead. Every direct cost must be in the budget. Hourly salary is annual salary divided by 1,642, at most 1,000 DKK. A request equal to 100% funding is ineligible.

Item 23 in full: Eurostars expects medical projects to start clinical trials after the project ends. Germany and Austria do not fund clinical-trial costs. IFD's pages say nothing either way, so the prospective study's fundability must be confirmed with IFD. IFD's English page also requires a product ready for market no later than 24 months after the project ends.

**Total 25. A stated 16, B stated 21.**

Context for A's count: A's founder never gave the budget split, ownership or accounts, and asked a different second question. A could not apply the rules to his numbers the way B could. Even so, A's general 70% statement would have shown him that a 22% hospital share fails.

## 5. Best recommendation: B (narrowly)

Both say to prepare for Call 12 (deadline 4 March 2027) rather than apply now or give up. The hidden profile's real blocker is the 70% rule, and B ties its recommendation to the founder's numbers:
- 78% is ineligible.
- Option A: ApS at most 510k EUR with the hospital at 220k (69.9%).
- Option B: add a Dutch company (65.2%), which also unlocks RVO funding for the hospital.
- Keeping 780k would need at least 335k EUR on the Dutch side.

The arithmetic is correct. B also flags a real financial risk: the undertaking-in-difficulty margin is only 520k DKK, and the 2026 accounts are the ones that will be checked.

A only states the 70% rule generically, although correctly for a two-partner consortium. A's strength is the clinical question. Following the Eureka FAQ and GBER Art. 2(86), A says not to count on Eurostars funding a confirmatory study on a locked algorithm: keep development and piloting inside the project, put the confirmatory study after it, and confirm with IFD. B misses exactly this. B's work packages ("WP3 Algoritmen låses", "WP5 Analyse og klinisk validering") build the project around the study, against the FAQ's expectation that clinical trials follow the project. B also never says that IFD funds only Danish activities.

I still give the edge to B. B resolves the blocker that would get the application rejected at the eligibility check. The clinical-scope issue is a scoring and fundability risk that IFD's rules leave open.
