## 1. Program

The founder chose **EuroHPC JU AI Factories Access Calls for Industrial Innovation, Fast Lane Access**, on LUMI-G through the LUMI AI Factory. In the portal it is call EHPC-AIF-2026FL (/calls/59), open from 2026-01-21 to 2026-12-31 22:59 UTC.

The trap holds. The EuroHPC access modes page and the LUMI access-calls page both say the Industrial Innovation modes are "open and free-of-charge to AI SMEs (including startups) for innovation purposes", and other industrial applications pay through commercial access. The LUMI AI Factory pricing page says "Free of charge services are not available to Large companies".

Under the EU SME definition, a firm in a larger group must count the group's staff, turnover and balance sheet. The ToR section 2(e) applies the Horizon 2020 rules of participation. Puhekaari is a 100% subsidiary of a listed group with 2,400 employees and 410M EUR turnover, so it is not an SME.

There is one nuance. The LUMI AI Factory's own Fast Lane page says access is "Free of charge for large businesses if they do open science and publish the results", and otherwise available for a fee. Puhekaari's goal is a commercial launch on the parent's streaming service, using speech licensed from the parent's archive. That is not open science, so the free track is effectively closed to it.

The ToR also says the hosting entity's terms prevail over the ToR when the two differ. LUMI's own terms are the narrower ones.

**Next step the funder points to:** pay-per-use commercial access to LUMI through CSC. It is currently open to Finnish companies only. It needs a CSC contract and payment by the service price list: LUMI-G costs 0.535 EUR per GPU-hour, project setup costs 1,000 EUR, and storage costs 0.005 EUR per TiB-hour. A free Try&Buy test project is available first. 30,000 GPU-hours would cost about 16,050 EUR plus setup and storage.

The LUMI AI Factory also points large companies to collaborative R&D projects funded by the EU or a national agency.

## 3. Factual errors

**A**
- Turn 2: "Puhekaari Oy, Suomi, 12 henkeä: LUMI AI Factoryn ilmaisten palvelujen yhtiöehdot täyttyvät." Under the hidden profile, the group-aggregated SME test fails, and the LUMI AIF pricing page says free services are not available to large companies. A decided this from headcount alone and never asked about turnover, balance sheet or ownership.
- Turn 2: "Fast Lane -sivulla on tänään vain yläraja, 50 000 GPU-tuntia. LUMI-G:n alarajaa en löytänyt." The Fast Lane systems table, which is an image, shows LUMI-G at "50 000 (Min. 10 000)". A's turn-1 range of 10,000-50,000 was correct, and this "correction" made it wrong. It does not matter for this founder, because 30,000 is above the minimum.
- Turn 2: "Sivun Apply Now -nappi vie suljettuun 2025-kutsuun (/calls/49)." Today the APPLY NOW button on the Fast Lane page links to the portal root, https://access.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/. A's other portal claims check out against the official portal API: call 59 runs from 2026-01-21 to 2026-12-31 22:59 UTC, and call 49 closed on 2025-12-31.

**B**
- None found. LUMI-G is indeed closed for new Large Scale allocations. The ToR and LUMI terms points are confirmed: host terms prevail, users are liable for costs even after the account closes, and organisation e-mail is required. So are the EuroHPC Federation Platform cutover on 15.4.2026, data deletion after 90 days, the 300,000 EUR state-aid cap, and the third-party datasets that are invoiced directly.
- B's turn-2 headline, "laskenta-ajasta ei tule laskua" (no bill for compute time), is wrong for this founder in substance. B qualifies it right away: free access depends on the eligibility conditions, and the first condition it lists is SME status counting the owners. So I do not count it as an error.
- The claim about a 2025 Council working-party discussion of state aid is supported by Council working document WK-1754-2025.

## 5. Best recommendation: B

- **What fits the hidden profile:** do not apply for the free track as an SME or startup. Go to paid commercial access through CSC, or to another funded route.
- **A** tells the founder to send the LUMI form now with organisation type "Start-up" and to finish a Fast Lane application. That recommendation is wrong for this founder.
- **B** makes free access conditional on SME status including owners, and tells the founder to confirm in writing with the LUMI AI Factory that Fast Lane is fully free for them before applying. It also names CSC paid access as the alternative. B is closer to right.
