## 1. Program

The program is the EIC Accelerator under Horizon Europe, EIC Work Programme 2026. This founder is at Step 2, the full proposal, for EIC Accelerator Open with blended finance (grant component plus investment component). The "SME Instrument phase 2" the founder mentions was replaced by the EIC Accelerator.

## 2. Stopper list

The trap holds. WP2026 (Accelerator, "Can you apply?", point c) allows a company from a non-associated third country to send the short proposal. That company "must prove its effective establishment in a Member State or an Associated Country at the time of submission of the full proposal". Footnote 102 adds that "the applicant must use the same identification (PIC number) when submitting the full application". The call page says relocation "must be done before submitting the full application (Step 2)", and the FAQ says the same twice.

The GO belongs to the Delaware Inc.'s PIC. So the Inc. cannot file an eligible full proposal on 4 November unless it is effectively established in the EU first. The rules also give no route for the Swedish AB to use the Inc.'s GO under its own PIC. The FAQ says the EU subsidiary "is eligible to apply itself", but that means a new application of its own. No 2027 cut-off is published: the FAQ and WP say 2027 dates will be "established in the 2027 EIC Work Programme". The funder's pages point to relocation inside the same program, not to another program, so `next_step_program` is null.

## 3. Factual errors

**A**
- Turn 2 says the EIC FAQ allows equity to "co-finance the project", presented as a source for the EUR 1.07-1.15M own share. WP2026 p.75 says the 30% co-funding of grant work packages "has to be financed by the beneficiary through its own resources". The Guide p.16 says a work package cannot be funded by a mixture of grant and investment, with the 70/30 split as its own example.

**B**
- Turn 1, minor and irrelevant to this founder: "A running EIC project blocks this one." The FAQ table lets beneficiaries of ongoing projects apply in several cases. For example, an ongoing Horizon Europe grant-only project may apply for blended finance or equity only.
- Turn 1 told the founder to "treat 4 November as your date" and to ask whether the GO carries over. The call page quote was accurate, but the FAQ and WP already allowed 2027. B corrected this itself in turn 2, so it is not counted as an error.

## 5. Best recommendation: B

The hidden profile makes the 4 November submission ineligible with the Inc. as applicant. The right advice is not to submit on 4 November, to get the EIC's or NCP's position in writing, and to restructure so that an EU or associated-country company holds the application before Step 2. The founder should then target a 2027 cut-off once one is published.

B gives exactly this:
- the rule and the same-PIC footnote
- an email to the named Vinnova NCPs
- the relocation ("make the AB the top company") before Step 2
- a go/no-go date of 28 October
- the first 2027 date, without inventing one

A also says not to submit on 4 November, but for readiness and IP reasons, and it anchors on an untraceable 3 Feb 2027 date. Three parts of A's answer assume the AB can apply under a GO tied to the Inc.'s PIC, which footnote 102 does not support:
- "the Swedish AB can still apply"
- the 3 Feb plan
- option 3, which keeps the Delaware structure, licenses the IP to the AB and applies for the grant only

## 6. Draft: B

The founder asked for the impact and implementation parts.

B's draft follows the official Part B numbering and questions:
- 2.1 Market opportunity, 2.2 Business and revenue model and growth strategy, 2.3 Broader impact
- 3.1 Team capability, with governance, incentives and gender balance as the template asks
- 3.2 Risk level of the investment and leverage effect
- 3.3 Risk management

B's implementation plan follows the template's split into grant work packages up to TRL 8 and investment work packages, with TRL, funding source and months. The budget:
- applies the 70% rate and 25% indirect correctly
- flags depreciation
- keeps the 30% as own resources and enforces the no-mixing rule (certification stays in the grant)
- converts SEK salaries into person-months per work package with correct arithmetic

Every gap is a [FILL IN], and proposals are labelled. B also adds the mandatory AI disclosure.

A's draft has more substance:
- problem and value text
- a bottom-up market table
- competition
- a work-package table with deliverables and milestones
- a 9-row risk table with owners
- correct per-work-package lump sums

But A admits it did not map to the 2026 form, and it uses its own structure (2.1-2.6, 3.1-3.4). It routes the own share to equity, against the rules. It also states unbracketed facts the founder never gave, such as a per-vessel subscription model with fouling trend reports, a second business line that "owners, insurers and cleaning companies pay for", and the Port of Gothenburg as a reference customer. Neither conversation drafted for a program the founder is permanently ruled out of: relocation keeps the EIC Accelerator open.

## 8. Verdict: B

B found the stopper that decides this case. The Delaware Inc. holds the GO, it must be EU-established under the same PIC by the full proposal, and no 2027 date exists yet. B gave the founder the exact rule, the NCP contacts and a restructuring path, and it covered 14 of 16 stoppers. Its draft follows the official form and stays within the founder's facts.

A was strong on budget mechanics, EIC Fund rules and IP. But it treated the applicant-entity problem only as a "check", built its plan on an untraceable 2027 date, misstated the own-share rule and added unsupported facts to its draft.
