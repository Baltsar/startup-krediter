# r03 grading report

Graded 2026-10-19 against official pages fetched today: naiss.se, supr.naiss.se, hpc.pages.naiss.se, nsc.liu.se, c3se.chalmers.se, swedenaifactory.se, eurohpc-ju.europa.eu (Fast Lane pages, Terms of Reference V3 of 31/03/2026, Access Policy v2025.12), the EuroHPC access portal and its public calls API, and kth.se (PDC).

Note on the transcripts: the founder's second turn differs between the two conversations. In A the founder asks whether they can apply now and start in January 2027. In B the founder asks whether an AB can apply, what it takes, what it costs and how long access takes. Stoppers are counted against one list regardless, but this explains why A spends its second turn on timing and B on requirements.

## 1. Program

The founder asked for "SNIC" compute on Berzelius or Alvis. SNIC was replaced by NAISS (National Academic Infrastructure for Supercomputing in Sweden) on 1 January 2023 (PDC news, 12 Jan 2023). Alvis is a NAISS resource that is closed to new allocations. Berzelius is not a NAISS system: SUPR describes it as a direct donation from the Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation to NSC, applied for through SUPR's "LiU Berzelius 2026" round.

The trap holds. NAISS gives compute free of charge only to researchers at Swedish universities and research institutes. Small needs a PI who is at least a doctoral student with an appointment of at least 20%. Medium and Large need at least an assistant professor with at least 60%. Berzelius needs a PI who is at least an assistant professor with a KAW affiliation. Nobody in the company holds such a position, so the founder is ruled out of the chosen program.

NAISS's own About page names the next step for companies. NAISS runs Sweden AI Factory with RISE, and that factory gives companies free GPU access through the EuroHPC AI Factory calls. For 20-30k GPU hours the matching call is EuroHPC AI Factories Fast Lane Access, EHPC-AIF-2026FL. The portal API shows that call open from 2026-01-21 to 2026-12-31. The Fast Lane systems table lists six systems, each with a 50,000 maximum and a 10,000 minimum: JUPITER Booster, Arrhenius GPU (NAISS, Sweden), MareNostrum5 ACC, Leonardo Booster, LUMI-G and MeluXina GPU. The page also says MareNostrum5 ACC and JUPITER Booster are currently unavailable.

## 3. Factual errors

A: one minor point. In turn 1, A says Alvis is being replaced by Arrhenius, "som också är akademiskt", and that the founder will probably run on another EuroHPC system somewhere in Europe. Today's Fast Lane systems table lists Arrhenius GPU, hosted by NAISS in Linköping, as a Fast Lane system, so a Swedish system is open to this founder. This is misleading rather than a wrong figure. A also says in turn 1 that no start date has been published for the new Sweden AI Factory computer. The FAQ gives an expected "early 2027", which A itself repeats in turn 2. I do not count that as an error.

Everything else in A checks out against official pages: SNIC replaced on 1 January 2023; the NAISS Small and Medium PI levels; the Berzelius conditions; Alvis closed; free for AI SMEs and startups for innovation, with paid commercial access otherwise; Playground 5,000 GPU-h within 2 working days; Fast Lane up to 50,000 GPU-h, approval within 4 working days, 1 to 3 months; Large Scale with two cut-offs a month for 3 to 12 months; no extension and no extra hours; PI employed for 3 more months; incomplete technical data rejected; MareNostrum5 ACC and JUPITER Booster unavailable; PDC sells CPU and GPU hours to companies through business-unit@pdc.kth.se; Dardel ends under NAISS on 2027-01-01; info@swedenaifactory.se; the Sweden AI Factory computer expected early 2027.

B: two points.
- B says which systems are open, and the limit for each, "står inte på sidan". The Fast Lane page carries a systems table (as an image, max 50,000 and min 10,000 GPU-h per system) and a text note that MareNostrum5 ACC and JUPITER Booster are unavailable.
- B says Berzelius requires the PI to have their own Wallenberg grant. NSC also accepts other KAW affiliations, such as WASP faculty, Wallenberg Academy Fellows or SciLifeLab group leaders. This is minor and does not affect this founder.

Everything else in B checks out: the call open until 31 December 2026; the 2025 call closed 31 December 2025; the closed-call link behind Apply Now; NAISS Small reviewed weekly and Berzelius monthly; Alvis listed as retired by C3SE; NAISS and RISE run Sweden AI Factory; the 4-working-day target; the 50,000 cap; the VAT proof; professional e-mail; registration behind a reCAPTCHA (confirmed on the portal's registration page); English; civilian purpose and the AI Act; no double-awarding; the AUP; the final report including energy and CO2, publishable after a year; and aif.access@eurohpc-ju.europa.eu.

A2 stays "pass" for B. Neither of B's errors states a wrong amount, date or open status: the first says information is missing from a page, the second is about eligibility.

## 4. Untraceable figures

A: A's timeline has the founder submit Fast Lane on Monday 4 January 2027 and expect access around 8-12 January. That assumes a Fast Lane call is open on 4 January. The open call EHPC-AIF-2026FL ends 2026-12-31, and no 2027 call is published. At the last changeover, the 2025 call closed on 2025-12-31 and the 2026 call opened only on 2026-01-21, so for three weeks no Fast Lane call was open. Following A's date as written could cost the founder most of January. To A's credit, its draft e-mail to Sweden AI Factory asks whether the Fast Lane call continues over the year-end without a break, and A says to follow the factory's answer.

B: none. Every date and figure B gives traces to the portal, the Terms of Reference, the Access Policy, NAISS or SUPR.

## 5. Best recommendation: A

Both conversations reach the right answer on the chosen program: NAISS, Berzelius and Alvis are closed to this company, so go to the next step the funder points to, Sweden AI Factory and EuroHPC Fast Lane.

The hidden profile needs compute from January to the end of March 2027. A is the only conversation that deals with that. It says not to submit now, because a 1-3 month allocation granted now would run out before January. It suggests testing on Playground in November and asking Sweden AI Factory first about a January start and about the call over the year-end. B tells the founder to apply in the 2026 call, open until 31 December, without asking when they need the compute. Applied now, that allocation would be used up before January.

A's specific submission date of 4 January carries the call-gap risk described in section 4. The better plan, which neither conversation states, is to submit in late December 2026, inside the open call, so that a 3-month allocation starts in early January. Even so, A's recommendation fits this founder better.
