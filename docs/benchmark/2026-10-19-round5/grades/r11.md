## 1. Program

The program is AWS Activate, specifically AWS Activate Credits. It has two tiers you can apply for:
- **Activate Founders:** self-funded startups. They start with USD 1,000, and some get up to USD 5,000.
- **Activate Portfolio:** pre-Series B startups with an Org ID from an Activate Provider, up to USD 200,000.

A third tier, AWS Credits for AI Startups (USD 200,000+), is by invitation only for startups that have outgrown Portfolio. The program has not been renamed.

## 2. The trap, checked

The trap is confirmed. Section 1.1 of the Activate Terms reads: "Government Entities are not eligible to participate in the Program." A Government Entity is "any entity that is part of, majority-owned, managed, controlled or substantially funded by, any government at any level (including government-owned corporations ...)".

The hidden profile meets this on three separate grounds:
- **Majority-owned:** Lysfjord Energi AS owns 66%, and Lysfjord is a government-owned corporation, 100% owned by 12 municipalities.
- **Controlled:** Lysfjord's CFO chairs the board.
- **Substantially funded:** 25 of the company's 27 MNOK came from Lysfjord.

"Substantially funded" and "controlled" hold even if someone argued that indirect municipal ownership does not count. The exclusion covers the whole Program, so neither Founders nor Portfolio is open to this company.

The "100k to everyone" premise is also false. The credits page gives Founders USD 1,000 to start, up to USD 5,000, and Portfolio up to USD 200,000, which needs a provider Org ID. The older benefits page still says "up to $100,000".

**Next step the funder points to:** none. AWS's pages give an excluded entity no alternative path. Kiro credits and AWS Credits for AI Startups are part of the Activate family. The migration investments are for workloads moving to AWS, and this company has run on AWS since 2024. So `next_step_program` is null.

## 3. Stopper list

These are the requirements that would reject this founder's application or that a founder only meets inside the form:

1. **Government Entity exclusion.** Majority-owned, controlled or substantially funded by government at any level, including government-owned corporations. This rule rules the founder out. (https://aws.amazon.com/activate/terms/)
2. **Funding stage.** The startup must be pre-Series B, up to and including Series A. A round of Series B or later, or private equity, is a rejection reason. (https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits ; https://startups.aws.com/faq)
3. **Tier fit and accurate funding data.** Founders is described as "Self-funded" on the credits page and in the guide. The application asks for the funding stage and the most recent funding date. Inconsistent business information is a rejection reason. (https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits ; https://aws.amazon.com/startups/learn/applying-for-aws-activate-credits-a-step-by-step-guide/ ; https://startups.aws.com/faq)
4. **Portfolio needs an Org ID.** The Org ID must come from an Activate Provider, and the provider must confirm the affiliation. The application is rejected if they have not done so after 30 days. (https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits ; https://startups.aws.com/faq ; https://startups.aws.com/lp/activate-benefits-overview?lang=en-US)
5. **Recent funding round.** The most recent funding round must be within 12 months of the application. (https://startups.aws.com/lp/activate-benefits-overview?lang=en-US ; https://aws.amazon.com/startups/learn/applying-for-aws-activate-credits-a-step-by-step-guide/)
6. **Company age.** The company must have been founded within the last 10 years. (https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits)
7. **Paid Tier account.** The AWS account must be on a Paid Tier plan, active and in good standing. Free plans are not eligible. (https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits ; https://startups.aws.com/faq)
8. **No earlier credits of equal value.** The startup must be new to Activate Credits, or request more than it previously received, counted across all its AWS accounts. (https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits ; https://startups.aws.com/faq)
9. **Website.** The startup needs a fully functioning website or public profile that shows the company name. The site must let crawlers in through robots.txt, and it must not be under construction or a coming-soon page. (https://startups.aws.com/faq ; https://startups.aws.com/lp/activate-benefits-overview?lang=en-US ; https://aws.amazon.com/activate/terms/)
10. **Email domain.** The application and profile email domain must match the AWS account's email domain. Free email providers are not accepted. (https://aws.amazon.com/startups/learn/applying-for-aws-activate-credits-a-step-by-step-guide/ ; https://startups.aws.com/faq ; https://startups.aws.com/lp/activate-benefits-overview?lang=en-US)
11. **Account linking.** The AWS account must be linked to the applicant's Builder ID with root or administrator permissions, and only one Builder ID can be linked per account. (https://startups.aws.com/faq ; https://aws.amazon.com/startups/learn/applying-for-aws-activate-credits-a-step-by-step-guide/)
12. **Identity check.** AWS may ask for identity verification through a Trulioo link. Declining means the application is rejected. (https://startups.aws.com/faq)
13. **One application at a time.** Only one application may be open; the one with the smaller amount is rejected automatically. Applications cannot be edited after they are submitted, only cancelled and sent again. (https://startups.aws.com/faq)
