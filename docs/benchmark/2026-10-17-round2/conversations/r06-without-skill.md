# r06 without-skill

## Founder, turn 1
Hi, we're a seed-funded HR tech startup (Delaware C-corp, team in SF) and my cofounder is Swedish. We'd love to tap into Vinnova's Innovative Startups grant to fund our European expansion. Can we apply since he's a Swedish citizen? We don't have a Swedish entity yet but could set one up if needed.

## Assistant, turn 1
Short answer: your cofounder's citizenship doesn't matter. Vinnova looks at the company that applies, not at who owns it. As you're set up today, you can't apply. A new Swedish subsidiary could apply in principle, but it probably won't pay for the expansion you have in mind.

**What Innovative Startups 2026 requires** (from Vinnova's call page):
- The applicant must be a Swedish limited company (aktiebolag, AB) that runs activities in Sweden. A Delaware C-corp is out, even with a Swedish founder.
- The company must be at most 10 years old on the decision date (23 March 2027), with at most 10 full-time employees.
- Net turnover and balance sheet total must not *both* be above SEK 10M (about $1M) in the last financial year. No profits paid out, and not listed.
- **The group rule is the catch for you.** If the AB belongs to a group, the whole group must meet the size limits. A Swedish subsidiary of your C-corp would be judged on the C-corp's numbers too. If your seed round left more than about SEK 10M on the balance sheet, check whether turnover is also above the limit. If both are, the group fails.
- The project leader must be employed by the Swedish AB. That probably means your cofounder, on Swedish payroll.
- The grant is up to SEK 500,000 (about $45-50k), can cover up to 100% of costs, and runs for up to 9 months.
- Timing: applications close 12 January 2027 at 14:00, the decision comes 23 March 2027, and projects start by April 2027. You have time to set up an AB (registration with Bolagsverket usually takes a few weeks, plus SEK 25,000 in share capital). Leave margin, though.

**Does it fit what you want?** Not really. The grant pays for early, innovation-heavy work: testing customer need, the business model and willingness to pay, for a new idea that's hard to copy. A Swedish AB could use it to validate your product with Swedish or Nordic customers. It's not money for opening a European market or hiring a sales team. Evaluators also want to see that the knowledge and the value stay in the Swedish company, not passed straight up to a US parent.

**Questions that would settle it:**
1. Roughly what are your balance sheet total and annual revenue right now?
2. Would the Swedish AB do real product or R&D work, with your cofounder (and maybe others) employed there? Or would it mainly be a sales office?
3. Is "European expansion" mostly go-to-market, or is there an R&D or new-product part to it?

Once I know that, I can tell you whether Innovative Startups is worth a try. If it isn't, there are better options for a US company expanding into Sweden and the EU: Almi (loans), Business Sweden (market entry help), and EU programs open to companies with an EU entity.

## Founder, turn 2 (simulated)

## Assistant, turn 2
Thanks, that settles it. Innovative Startups won't work for you, and setting up a Swedish company won't change that.

**Why it's a no:**
- **You fail the group size test on both numbers.** A Swedish subsidiary would be judged on your whole group. $1.2M ARR is roughly SEK 11-13M in turnover, above the SEK 10M cap. A $4M seed means a balance sheet total of around SEK 40M, far above the cap. The rule allows one figure over the limit, not both.
- **The project doesn't match the call.** The grant pays for testing a new, hard-to-copy idea in the Swedish company: customer need, business model, willingness to pay. A sales office doing go-to-market for a US product with traction is outside that. Evaluators would reject it even if the numbers passed.
- **No one is employed in Sweden.** The project leader has to work for the Swedish company.

**What I'd do instead:**
- **Business Sweden's investment-promotion team** helps foreign companies set up in Sweden for free: entity setup, hiring, tax and Sweden-specific market info. It's the closest thing to public support for what you're actually doing. [business-sweden.com](https://www.business-sweden.com)
- **Assume no grants will pay for this.** Neither Sweden nor the EU gives grants for a sales office or market entry by a funded, revenue-generating company. Most EU innovation money (EIC Accelerator, Eurostars) also requires real R&D. Your seed round and revenue are the realistic funding.
- **If you later build product in Sweden,** the picture changes. Say your cofounder moves back and leads real development of something new there. Some Vinnova calls that don't have the startup size caps could then apply. That's worth revisiting only if R&D actually moves there.

One practical note: talk to an accountant who knows both US and Swedish rules before you set up the AB. That decision is mostly about tax and employment, not grants. Transfer pricing between the C-corp and the AB, and employer obligations once you hire in Sweden, matter far more than SEK 500k ever would.

If you'd like, I can sketch the usual setup choices for a US company hiring its first person in Sweden (AB subsidiary, branch, or an employer-of-record service) and what each one costs.
