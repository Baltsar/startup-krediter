# s13 without-skill

## Founder, turn 1
Hi! We're a battery materials startup in Västerås, AB founded 2023, 11 people. We want to apply to the EIC Accelerator for the full €2.5M grant plus the equity part to build our pilot line, total need is about €9M. Is the short application open right now and what are our chances? Also can we stack it with Swedish Energy Agency money?

## Assistant, turn 1
Short version: yes, you can submit the short application now. But the EIC won't cover the full €9M on its own. Swedish Energy Agency (Energimyndigheten) money can sit alongside it, as long as it pays for different costs.

**1. Is the short application open?**
Yes. You can submit a short proposal at any time. The EIC evaluates them in monthly batches, starting the first Tuesday of each month at 17:00 Brussels time. Results take about 4 to 6 weeks.
- The next batch starts Tuesday 3 November 2026, so you'd hear back in early to mid-December.
- That puts the last 2026 full-proposal cut-off (4 November) out of reach. The exception is if you already got a GO in the past 12 months. A GO lets you submit the full proposal to any cut-off within 12 months.
- Your realistic first full-proposal cut-off is early 2027. The 2027 dates haven't been published yet.
- After that, jury interviews only happen after every second batch. The grant agreement takes about 3 months and the equity due diligence 2 to 6 months. Realistically the money arrives in late 2027, so plan a bridge.
- The EIC switches to its 2027 programme soon. Before you submit, check on the Funding & Tenders portal that the current short-application topic still accepts proposals.

**2. What "€2.5M grant plus equity" means in practice**
- **Grant:** a lump sum of up to €2.5M that covers 70% of costs. A full grant therefore means about €3.6M of work at TRL 6 to 8, finished within 24 months. You pay the other 30%.
- **Equity:** €1M to €10M through the EIC Fund, so asking for about €6.5M is within range. Being selected doesn't guarantee it, though:
  - The fund runs 2 to 6 months of due diligence and invests at a valuation, so you get diluted.
  - If it decides you're not ready for investment, you start with the grant only. The equity is looked at again at agreed milestones.
- **Pilot-line equipment:** check early how much of the line the grant can actually pay for. Horizon Europe rules usually count equipment as depreciation during the project, not the purchase price. Expect the equipment-heavy part to be paid with equity and private money.
- **In practice:** plan the €9M as a €2.5M grant plus EIC equity plus private co-investors, not €9M from Brussels.

**3. Your chances**
The EIC's own numbers:
- **October 2025 cut-off:** 923 full proposals. 121 reached interview and 61 were funded, about 7% of full proposals.
- **January and March 2026 cut-offs:** 87 reached interview and 38 were funded.

So roughly half of the companies interviewed get funded, but reaching the interview is the hard part, and you have to pass the short proposal before that.

I can't judge your own odds yet. Since Northvolt collapsed, evaluators and investors look hard at Swedish battery projects. They'll focus on three things:
- samples validated by cell makers
- cost compared with Chinese suppliers
- real customer demand

Those decide it more than how well the application is written.

There is one possible advantage. The 2026 programme has an EIC Challenge called "Advanced Materials for Renewable Energy and Energy Storage Systems". There is also one on critical raw materials. Each challenge has its own budget of €20M to €50M. The 2027 challenges haven't been announced. If an energy-storage materials challenge comes back, it's a smaller, more targeted pool worth aiming for.

**4. Combining it with Energimyndigheten money**
Yes, you can, but not for the same costs.
- **The rule:** Energimyndigheten's call text requires you to declare any Horizon Europe funding for the same costs. It refers to EU state-aid rules (GBER Article 8.2). Under those rules, total public funding on the same costs can't exceed the EU programme's rate. For the EIC grant that rate is 70%, and the grant already uses all of it. So Swedish grants can't cover your 30% share.
- **What works:** split the costs, for example separate work packages or a separate demo project, or run the projects one after the other. Agree the split with an Energimyndigheten case officer before you apply to both.

What's available on the Swedish side:
- **Pilot och demonstration (Energimyndigheten):**
  - The 2026 round closed on 1 June, and the next is planned for Q1 2027.
  - You need at least SEK 10M of eligible costs and TRL 5 to 8.
  - A customer who sets requirements must take part and sign a letter of intent.
  - Small companies get at most 45%.
  - It gives no investment support. Equipment only counts as depreciation while it's used in the project.
- **Vinnova, "Scaling up for a sustainable battery value chain 2026":**
  - It closed on 24 March 2026 and gave up to SEK 20M per project. No new round has been announced.
  - It doesn't allow other public funding for the same project costs.

**A possible shortcut:** Vinnova's Innovationsprojekt i företag (IPF) and Innovativa startups are certified EIC "Plug-In" programmes. If you've had one of those projects, Vinnova may be able to send you straight to the full proposal and skip the short one. Vinnova decides that, not you, so ask them.

**To judge your chances properly, tell me:**
1. What material and process is it, and what TRL are you at? Has a cell maker or carmaker tested your samples?
2. What customer commitments do you have: letters of intent, joint development agreements, purchase agreements?
3. How much private money have you raised, and is there an investor ready to invest alongside the EIC?
4. Have you applied to the EIC before? Have you had Vinnova or Energimyndigheten projects?
5. How does the €9M split between development work and equipment for the line?

## Assistant, turn 2
Thanks, that helps a lot. The ownership changes the answer, so I'll start there.

**1. Nordvik's 62% very likely shuts you out of the EIC Accelerator**
- The EIC Accelerator is for start-ups and SMEs as the EU defines them. Under that definition, if another company holds a majority of your voting rights, you are "linked". You must then add 100% of the whole group's staff and financials to your own.
- To count as an SME, you need under 250 staff and either turnover of €50M or less or a balance sheet of €43M or less. Those figures are counted for the whole group. A large listed industrial group is almost certainly over them, so on paper you are a large company, not an 11-person start-up.
- The EIC has one route for companies that aren't SMEs: "small mid-caps" with up to 499 employees. That route gives equity only, for scaling up something that's already at TRL 9, and no grant. Headcount there is almost certainly counted at group level too, so it is probably closed as well.
- Applying anyway risks rejection at the eligibility check. Even after an award, the EIC can end the grant if your ownership calls the award into question.
- **What could change this:** two things.
  - Nordvik's 62% might be capital but not a majority of votes, with no right to appoint most of the board. That's unlikely.
  - The group might actually be under the size limits.

  Dropping below 50% doesn't fix it on its own. A 25–50% holder makes you "partners", and you add their share of the group's numbers. For a large group, that usually still puts you over the limits. Only an owner below 25% gets you back in. That's a major ownership decision, not something to do just for an application.

My advice: don't spend money on an EIC application or consultant until your CFO has checked this against the group's annual report.

**2. Check your 2024 Energimyndigheten project**
Energimyndigheten sets aid levels by company size, using the same EU definition, owners included. For experimental development, a small company can get up to 45% and a large one 25%. For industrial research it's 70% versus 50%.
- Check two things:
  - what company size you declared in the 2024 application
  - when Nordvik's stake went above 25% and above 50%
- If you declared yourselves small while Nordvik already held a large stake, you may have received more aid than allowed. If so, raise it with Energimyndigheten yourselves before you apply again.

**3. What this means for the €9M**
- **Nordvik is the natural funder.** With 62%, the pilot line is in practice a group investment decision. Public money can add to it but won't pay for most of it.
- **Energimyndigheten, Pilot och demonstration** (next round planned for Q1 2027):
  - As a large company you get at most 25% of eligible costs.
  - It gives no investment aid. Equipment only counts as depreciation while it's used in the project.
  - You need at least SEK 10M of eligible costs, and you must have completed TRL 5–8 with evidence.
  - A customer who sets requirements must take part and sign a letter of intent.
- **EU Innovation Fund:**
  - It's open to companies of any size, and unlike the options above it can pay for investment costs.
  - Its next call for net-zero technologies is expected to open in December 2026, with a deadline around April 2027. Those dates come from consultancies and aren't officially confirmed yet.
  - It has categories for clean-tech manufacturing and for pilots. Check whether your material counts.
  - Competition is tough. The last round asked for €17.5B against a €2.9B budget.
- **Combining sources:** the same rule as before applies. You can't take two public grants for the same costs beyond the allowed level.

**4. Placing your TRL**
A rough guide for battery materials:
- **TRL 4:** the material works in lab cells.
- **TRL 5:** made with your scalable process and tested in cells under realistic conditions.
- **TRL 6:** pilot-scale material tested by a customer in prototype cells.
- **TRL 7:** the customer is qualifying it in production-format cells.

Your own pilot reactors and paid sample batches suggest roughly TRL 5–6. What decides it is what your paying customers did with the samples. Ask them for written test results. That gives you evidence of your TRL and is also the first step toward a letter of intent.

**What I need from you next:**
1. What exactly does Nordvik hold: what share of the votes, and how many board seats? What are the group's headcount and turnover?
2. Which company size did you declare in the 2024 Energimyndigheten project?
3. Who bought the sample batches, and what did they test them in? Can you get written results?
4. Has Nordvik said whether it will fund the pilot line, and how much?
