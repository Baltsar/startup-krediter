TURN 2:
Here is Business Finland's Sprint grant in detail, based on the current call page.

**Money**
- The project budget must be €100,000–133,334, and Business Finland pays 75% of it. So you get €75k–100k and fund the other 25% yourselves.
- It's a de minimis grant and doesn't have to be repaid.
- You're under five years old, so 70% is paid in advance. The rest is paid after the project ends, based on approved costs.

**Basic criteria, checked against your company**
| Requirement | You |
|---|---|
| Finnish Oy with a Business ID, operating in Finland | Yes |
| Small company: under 50 employees, turnover or balance sheet ≤ €10M | Yes |
| Under 5 years old (counted from Trade Register entry), no previous Business Finland funding | Probably yes. Confirm you've had no Tempo or other BF funding |
| Team of at least 2 working in Finland, at least 1 full time | Yes, 2 full time |
| **≥ €50,000 equity on the balance sheet** | **Unknown. This is the critical one** |
| **Net financial assets (cash + short-term receivables − short-term liabilities) ≥ €63,334** | **Unknown** |

On the equity requirement, these count: share capital, SVOP, qualifying capital loans, and investor commitments backed by a signed commitment or term sheet. Money you've already raised counts even if you've spent it. If you're short, the usual fixes are to put founder money into SVOP or to get a signed angel or pre-seed term sheet before you apply.

**What the money can pay for**
- At least two thirds of costs must be R&D, for example building the MVP or prototype, customer pilots, R&D planning, and IP assessment or a first patent filing.
- The rest can go to customer and market validation, for example demand testing, market surveys, competitor analysis, and preparing a funding round.
- It can't pay for sales, marketing, operations, production investments, staff training, or export and distribution costs.
- Costs only count after you submit the application. Don't sign binding purchases before that. Any purchase of €60k or more must be competitively tendered.

**How it's judged**
1. **Team:** complementary skills, commitment, and a plan to strengthen the team.
2. **Innovation:** a concrete solution with an international competitive edge, defined target customers, a preliminary revenue model, and adequate IP.
3. **Market:** international potential with some early evidence behind it, and a realistic view of market size and entry requirements.
4. **Project plan:** written in Business Finland's template, with measurable goals, a risk assessment, and the two-thirds R&D share.
5. **Early-stage logic:** the project validates technology, customer need and demand, and leads to larger R&D later.
6. **Follow-on plan:** a credible plan for further R&D and how you'll fund it.
7. **Financial resources:** enough money for the project and the rest of the business.

For construction logistics SaaS, the weak points are usually the market criterion and the early-stage logic. Show which countries you'd enter first, why the problem is the same there, and some proof from conversations, LOIs or pilots with contractors.

**How to apply**
1. Register in My Business Finland and fill in your company's background information.
2. Check the basic criteria, then book a meeting with a Business Finland advisor. You have to have this meeting before you apply.
3. Submit through the Funding e-service. It must be done by someone who can sign for the company alone, or who has Suomi.fi authorisation for company funding.
4. Required attachments:
   - the project plan in Business Finland's template
   - your shareholder list
   - an account-level income statement and balance sheet from your accounting system, no more than two months old
   - optionally, a pitch deck
5. Name a contact person and a project lead.

It's a continuous call with no deadline. Demand was high during the pilot, with over 200 applications, so the strength of your plan matters.

**Ways you'd be ruled out**
- previous Tempo, R&D, NIY, Deep Tech Accelerator or Sprint funding
- earlier funding for the same solution
- being a "company in difficulty" under the EU definition (for a young company, mainly whether losses have eaten up more than half of your equity)
- having used up your de minimis quota

**Next step:** check your equity and cash today against the €50k and €63,334 thresholds. If you meet them, book the advisor meeting. If you don't, close the gap first.
