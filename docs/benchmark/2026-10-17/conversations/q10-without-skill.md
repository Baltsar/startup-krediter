# q10 without-skill

## Founder, turn 1
Hi, I'm a postdoc at Chalmers (originally from Italy) working on integrated photonics for optical interconnects in data centers. We got a result this year that I think could become a company but I honestly have no idea how this works in Sweden. Is there funding for researchers who want to test if something is commercially viable before starting a company? And does it matter that I'm not Swedish, my work permit is tied to my postdoc position

## Assistant, turn 1
Yes. Sweden has funding for exactly this stage, and you're at a good university for it. Here's how it works, and a quick check on the permit question, because I think you may be better placed than you assume.

**1. Funding to test commercial viability (before you start a company)**

Your first stop is the **Chalmers Innovation Office**. It runs Vinnova's verification program for researchers, called VFT (Verifiering för tillväxt). The money is meant for the step you describe: checking whether a research result could become a business before anyone forms a company. It can pay for things like:
- customer and market interviews (for you, data-center operators, transceiver makers and hyperscalers)
- a patent search and an IP strategy
- prototype or demonstrator work to make the result more convincing
- business advisors and early contacts with investors

You don't apply to Vinnova yourself. Vinnova gives the money to university innovation offices, and they hand it out to their own researchers' projects. The office also coaches you, so the process is fairly informal: you contact them, explain the result, and they help you build a verification plan. Contact: chalmers.innovationskontor@chalmers.se. I couldn't find a current Chalmers-specific amount. Typical first tranches at Swedish universities run from tens of thousands of SEK up to a few hundred thousand, and good cases can get more later. Ask them directly.

After that, these usually come next:
- **Chalmers Ventures**: Chalmers' incubator and early investor. It's a common next step for deep-tech spinouts from Chalmers, and photonics fits well.
- **Vinnova's later startup programs and the EU's EIC instruments**: these are for once a company exists. I'd look at them later, not now.
- **ERC Proof of Concept**: only an option if your group leader holds an ERC grant that this result came from. Worth asking.

**2. Two things to do before anything else**
- **Don't publish or present the result yet.** Talk to the Innovation Office first. A publication or conference talk can destroy patentability, and investors in photonics hardware will care about IP.
- **Check who owns the result.** Sweden has the "teacher's exemption" (lärarundantaget), so researchers usually own their own results, not the university. That can change if the work was done under an industry collaboration, certain EU projects, or a funding agreement with special terms. The Innovation Office can check this with you.

**3. Does it matter that you're not Swedish?**
For the funding, no. VFT and Chalmers' support are based on your role at Chalmers, not your nationality.

For the permit, one key question: **do you hold Italian (EU) citizenship?** If you do, you shouldn't need a Swedish work permit at all. EU citizens have a right to live and work in Sweden, including running their own company, and that right isn't tied to your postdoc. The document you have may be something else, such as a registration or a contract-linked permit you got while holding another passport.

If you're *not* an EU citizen and you hold a residence permit for research, it gets more delicate:
- A research permit is tied to the research. Running a company on the side or leaving the postdoc can affect it.
- Migrationsverket has a permit for after finished research, which lets you stay to look for work or explore starting a business. You must apply before your current permit expires.
- Running your own company long term needs a separate self-employment permit with its own requirements.

In that case, do the verification phase while you're still employed (that's normal anyway) and get proper migration advice before you resign or found the company.

**To point you to the right next step, could you tell me:**
1. Your citizenship(s), and what kind of permit or document you actually hold?
2. Who funds the project the result came from (Swedish Research Council, an EU project, an industry partner, etc.)?
3. Have you published or presented the result anywhere yet?
4. What stage is it at: a lab demonstration, a device, or more of a design or simulation result?

## Founder, turn 2 (simulated)
1. Italian citizen, and I have a residence and work permit tied to my postdoc. 2. The research is funded by a Swedish Research Council grant held by my professor. 3. Not sure honestly. 4. It's a lab result, fabricated in the Chalmers cleanroom

## Assistant, turn 2
Thanks, that helps a lot. Here's what it means for each part.

**1. Your permit: as an Italian citizen, you almost certainly don't need it**
Migrationsverket says EU citizens can live and work in Sweden without a work or residence permit. That includes being self-employed or running your own company. Your right to stay comes from EU free movement ("right of residence"), not from the postdoc. So leaving the postdoc to run a company should not, in itself, cost you your right to stay.

There is one thing to clear up. Sweden doesn't normally issue work permits to EU citizens, so the document you have probably isn't one. My guess is one of these:
- a personnummer and population registration with Skatteverket (normal for EU citizens)
- a card or decision issued on a second, non-EU passport
- a document from Chalmers HR that describes the job, not a Migrationsverket permit

Check what the card or decision says and who issued it. If it's from Migrationsverket and lists a non-EU nationality, tell me, because then the picture changes. Otherwise, I'd stop treating the permit as a constraint. Ask Chalmers HR or Migrationsverket to confirm in one line.

**2. Who owns the result: likely you, but probably not only you**
- **Swedish Research Council grant:** good news. VR grants don't give VR the rights to results, so the teacher's exemption (researchers own their own results) should apply as normal.
- **Your professor:** this matters more. If your professor contributed to the idea, they are likely a co-inventor and co-owner. The same goes for any other group members who contributed. Talk to your professor early and openly. They are often your best ally: they know the field and can open doors, and many professors take a minority role or advisory seat in spinouts. You don't want it to come up after you've founded a company.
- **The cleanroom:** fabricating in the Chalmers cleanroom does not normally give anyone rights to your results. If anyone from an external company or another project contributed processes or designs, check whether there's an agreement.

**3. "Not sure" about publication: this is the most urgent thing to check**
Patents in Europe require "absolute novelty," with no grace period. If the result has been made public before you file, you may not be able to patent it. In photonics hardware, investors will care a lot about this. "Public" includes more than journal papers:
- conference talks, posters and abstracts (including ones only submitted and accepted)
- preprints (arXiv)
- theses and licentiate reports
- slides shared outside the group, and open seminars

Go through that list this week. Above all, **don't submit anything new until you've talked to the Innovation Office.** If something is already out, the case isn't necessarily dead. A patent can sometimes still cover an improvement or a variant, and some companies are built on know-how instead of patents.

**4. A lab result made in a cleanroom: a good fit for verification funding**
This is exactly the stage VFT funding is for. The usual questions at your stage:
- Can it be made at scale and at reasonable cost, ideally with CMOS-compatible or foundry processes?
- How does it compare with what transceiver makers and hyperscalers use today, in energy per bit, bandwidth density and reliability?
- Who would buy it first: a component maker, a module maker, or the data-center operator directly?

A verification project could fund a patent search and IP strategy, 15–20 interviews with potential customers, and possibly a second fabrication run to show reproducibility.

**Suggested next steps:**
1. **This week:** list everything that might have made the result public, and email the Chalmers Innovation Office (chalmers.innovationskontor@chalmers.se). Ask for a meeting about a possible invention and verification funding.
2. **At the same time:** talk to your professor about co-ownership and their role.
3. **Ongoing:** check your permit document, so you know whether you're truly free to start a company.
4. **Later, once the Innovation Office has seen it:** Chalmers Ventures, which incubates and invests in deep-tech spinouts.
