# Compute: free and subsidised GPU and HPC hours for Nordic companies and researchers

GPU hours are not money. An allocation on LUMI, Arrhenius or JUPITER lets you train or fine-tune a model on a supercomputer; it does not pay an OpenAI, Anthropic or cloud invoice, and you cannot serve a product from it (EuroHPC and LUMI AI Factory allocations are for research, development and innovation, with a final report at the end). For a company the main free door is the EuroHPC AI Factories Industrial Innovation track: Playground (5 000 GPU hours, rolling, access within two working days), Fast Lane (up to 50 000 GPU hours, rolling) and Large Scale (more than 50 000 GPU hours, two cut-offs a month, scored). Academia and the public sector go through AI for Science (20 000 to 90 000 node hours, six months) or their national allocation system (NAISS and SUPR in Sweden, Sigma2 in Norway). The national AI Factories (Sweden AI Factory, LUMI AI Factory, Sigma2's KI-fabrikken) mostly help you apply to the EuroHPC calls; only LUMI AI Factory in Finland has a separate national grant route. NVIDIA Inception is a perks program, not hours.

Not covered here: cloud vendor startup credits (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, those live in the cloud-credit reference), the Business Finland computing capacity grant itself (grants domain), EuroHPC Benchmark, Development, Regular and Extreme Scale access for traditional HPC, the Danish and Icelandic AI Factory antennas, and paid commercial access to LUMI or Sigma2.

All pages below were fetched on 2026-10-15 unless marked otherwise. Hour tables on the EuroHPC pages are images; they were downloaded and read the same day.

## EuroHPC AI Factories Playground Access {#eurohpc-playground}

- **Who:** Industry users, in particular European SMEs and startups that are new to HPC or have small compute needs; organisation established in an EU member state or a country associated to Horizon 2020, Horizon Europe or Digital Europe.
- **What:** A fixed, free allocation on one AI Factory partition (JUPITER Booster, Arrhenius GPU, MN5 ACC, Leonardo Booster, LUMI-G, Discoverer GPU, MeluXina GPU, Vega GPU) with guidance from the host, for AI work with a civilian purpose.
- **Amount:** 5 000 GPU hours fixed per application, 1, 2 or 3 months, one system only.
- **Requirements (Terms of Reference V3, 31/03/2026, section 2 and 4):** company, SME or startup; the project lead has an employment contract at submission that is valid for at least 3 months after the allocation ends; status proven with a valid VAT number (and PIC if applicable); all e-mail addresses used must be professional addresses; civilian purpose; compliance with the AI Act; proposal in English; no double-awarding with a running EuroHPC allocation.
- **How to apply:** Online form on https://access.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ (the page's Apply Now button links the portal root). Eligibility check by self-assessment plus ethics self-assessment, then technical assessment by the requested AI Factory, then first-come-first-served. Registration has a captcha the founder solves themselves; the founder clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/playground-access-ai-factories_en
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15 (page, Terms of Reference PDF and the hour-table image)
- **Evidence:** "Proposals are evaluated on first-come, first-served basis. The process comprises eligibility and technical assessment."
- **Gotchas:** The hour table is an image ("Systems available for AI Playground Access v2.png", September 2026): every partition shows 5 000 under FIXED ALLOCATION in GPU hours. Partition availability may be limited by demand; pick another if yours is unavailable. No extensions, no top-ups, one Playground award per project lead at a time. Final report three months after the end; results published except confidential parts; EuroHPC JU and the AI Factory must be acknowledged. "Innovation of the application will be the decisive factor of the final allocation decision." The portal is a JavaScript app, so the call number (/calls/60 from an earlier read) could not be confirmed today; see "Needs browser" below. Questions: aif.access@eurohpc-ju.europa.eu.

## EuroHPC AI Factories Fast Lane Access {#eurohpc-fast-lane}

- **Who:** SMEs and startups already familiar with HPC whose AI activity has innovative aspects and needs a medium-size allocation; same organisation and country rules as Playground.
- **What:** Up to 50 000 free GPU hours on one AI Factory system with expert support from the selected AI Factory, FIFO.
- **Amount:** Up to 50 000 GPU hours, 1, 2 or 3 months, one system only.
- **Requirements:** Same Terms of Reference as Playground: company, project lead employed until 3 months after the allocation, VAT number, professional e-mail, civilian purpose, AI Act, English, no double-awarding. "HPC-ready users".
- **How to apply:** Fast Lane call on the portal, https://access.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/calls/49 (the page's Apply Now link). Continuously open, no cut-offs, rolling assessment: eligibility check, technical assessment, first-come-first-served. The founder clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories/ai-factories-access-modes/fast-lane-access-ai-factories_en
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15
- **Evidence:** "This type of access is intended for applicants who are more familiar with HPC and requiring up to 50,000 GPU hours for a maximum of 3 months."
- **Gotchas:** Page promises "Approval within 4 working days". No per-system table on the page, only the 50 000 cap; the ToR says thresholds are "on the Fast Lane Access mode website". One Fast Lane award per project lead at a time; no extensions or top-ups; final report after three months.

## EuroHPC AI Factories Large Scale Access {#eurohpc-large-scale}

- **Who:** Industry organisations, SMEs and startups with high-impact, high-gain innovative AI work that needs a large allocation of compute, storage and support; same country rules.
- **What:** More than 50 000 free GPU hours on one AI Factory system for three, six or twelve months, after a scored expert evaluation.
- **Amount:** More than 50 000 GPU hours, 3, 6 or 12 months, one system. Per-system minimum and maximum requests are "listed in the Call text" on the portal; proposals below the minimum are administratively rejected. Not visible on the public page.
- **Requirements:** Same ToR eligibility as Playground, plus an online application demonstrating relevance, significant impact, the need for a large allocation, technical fit for the AI Factory (why a smaller system will not do), a resource schedule, and an ethics self-assessment.
- **How to apply:** Large Scale call on the portal, https://access.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/calls/48. Submit before a cut-off; two cut-offs a month. Eligibility check, technical assessment, expert evaluation by the AI Factory's Industrial Innovation Group on innovation and impact (each 0 to 5, threshold 3 per criterion and 6 overall, equal weight), ranked list approved by the EuroHPC JU Executive Director. The founder clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories/ai-factories-access-modes/large-scale-access-ai-factories_en
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15
- **Evidence:** "Large Scale access mode is for AI models and applications requiring more than 50,000 GPU hours for a maximum of one year."
- **Gotchas:** The page says "Approval within 10 working days" from the cut-off but lists no cut-off dates. The ToR recommends testing the production code via Playground first and allows awards "with a reduced scope". EIC Accelerator challenge awardees pass without evaluation and have priority. Late submissions roll into the next cut-off. Appeals within 15 days to aif.access@eurohpc-ju.europa.eu. Continuation proposals need a progress or final report.

## EuroHPC AI for Science and Collaborative EU Projects Access {#eurohpc-ai-for-science}

- **Who:** Scientific users from academia and research institutes and users from the public sector, established in an EU member state or an associated country. Industrial users only when they participate in an R&I project funded by Horizon Europe or Digital Europe; "All other types of industrial users should target the AI for Industrial Innovation Access modes."
- **What:** Free, guaranteed six-month access to one system (Arrhenius GPU, JUPITER Booster, MN5 ACC, Leonardo Booster, LUMI-G, MeluXina GPU) for scientific research that trains AI models, including LLMs, as part of the research workflow.
- **Amount:** 20 000 to 90 000 node hours on one system (image table, read 2026-10-15: minimum 20 000 and maximum 90 000 on every listed partition), six months.
- **Requirements (AI for Science Terms of Reference, 20/05/2025):** organisation in a member state or associated country; project lead with an employment contract valid at least 3 months after the allocation; professional e-mail addresses; civilian purpose; English; no double-awarding; application in two parts, an online form and a Project Scope and Plan document on the template (PDF), with a resource schedule and Gantt chart and an ethics self-assessment; at most 10 percent of the allocation for inference.
- **How to apply:** https://access.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/, call "AI for Science and Collaborative EU Projects". Administrative check, technical assessment by the hosting entity, peer review by two independent experts on excellence, innovation and impact, quality and efficiency of implementation (0 to 5 each, threshold 3 per criterion and 10 overall), then first-come-first-served until the cut-off's resources are depleted. The project lead clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/eurohpc-ju-call-proposals-ai-science-and-collaborative-eu-projects_en
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15 (page, ToR PDF, Full Call Details PDF, hour-table image)
- **Evidence:** "The call is continuously open, with pre-defined cut-off dates" and "The allocations are granted for six (6) months."
- **Gotchas:** Cut-offs on the page: 27 Feb, 30 Apr, 30 Jun, 1 Sep, 30 Oct, 11 Dec 2026 and 31 Dec 2033, 10:00 CET; the Full Call Details PDF says 31 August instead of 1 September. Next cut-off after today: 30 October 2026. Access starts at most one month after the cut-off. EU-funded projects already evaluated by expert panels skip the expert evaluation and have priority. Proposals not following the template are administratively rejected. Extension of up to three months on request at least one month before the end; more than 20 percent unused time is removed. The company-in-EU-project exception cannot be expressed as a matcher rule. Questions: access@eurohpc-ju.europa.eu.

## LUMI AI Factory (Finnish national track) {#lumi-ai-factory-finland}

- **Who:** Finnish startups and SMEs by the EU definition (fewer than 250 employees, turnover at most 50 million euro and/or balance sheet at most 43 million euro) doing AI research, development and innovation; public service and research organisations are also served. Not individuals, temporary teams, or large companies (no free services).
- **What:** Free LUMI computing capacity, datasets, consultation and training. Finnish companies can use both the EuroHPC JU calls and national routes (Business Finland computing capacity grant, nationally funded RDI projects with academia) or pay for commercial access.
- **Amount:** Not published. "We will help you estimate the amount of resources you need"; usually starts with a small computing pilot.
- **Requirements:** Officially established startup or SME, based in the EU or an affiliated country; resources for RDI purposes only; final report after an EU-funded computing project; the value of free services is reported as state aid.
- **How to apply:** Contact form at https://lumi-ai-factory.eu/contact/ (inquiry type, name, email, organisation, organisation type, country, domain area, message). The team proposes the route. The founder clicks submit on the form, later on the chosen application.
- **Status page:** https://lumi-ai-factory.eu/pricing-and-eligibility/
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15
- **Evidence:** "In Finland, it is possible to apply also for a Business Finland computing capacity grant, which is subject to state aid rules."
- **Gotchas:** Consortium countries are Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Norway and Poland (both EuroHPC and national routes); antennas Belgium, Iceland, Latvia, Switzerland; other EU and Horizon Europe associated countries only through EuroHPC calls. National route: "you are responsible for knowing that your company has not used up all national state aid available to companies (maximum 300.000 € in three years' time)." Commercial paid access "currently possible for Finnish companies". Datasets are free only within a research project granted LUMI resources. The FAQ points fully confidential projects to national mechanisms such as Business Finland. No hour caps or durations on the page.

## Sigma2 KI-fabrikken (Norwegian AI Factory) {#sigma2-ki-fabrikken}

- **Who:** Public institutions and Norwegian businesses, plus research environments.
- **What:** Norway's contribution to the LUMI AI Factory: access to national (Olivia) and European compute, model guidance for ML in HPC and GPU environments, onboarding, hosted open language models on national infrastructure, and private dedicated AI instances.
- **Amount:** Not published on the page.
- **Requirements:** None stated on the page; "Ta kontakt" (get in touch).
- **How to apply:** Contact form at the bottom of https://www.sigma2.no/nb/ki-fabrikken (name, email, phone, message) or contact@sigma2.no. The founder clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://www.sigma2.no/nb/ki-fabrikken
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15 (Norwegian page; the English link /node/406 serves the same Norwegian text)
- **Evidence:** "Vi tilbyr offentlige institusjoner og norske virksomheter tilgang til nasjonal og europeisk datakraft, ekspertveiledning og tjenester tilpasset norske krav til personvern og etterlevelse av AI Act."
- **Gotchas:** Ordinary national allocations (sigma2.no/apply-e-infrastructure-resources) run through metacenter.no with a call in January (allocations from 1 April) and June (from 1 October), deadlines usually February and August, open to "Industrial parties", but the user contribution model (prices from 1 October 2025) puts commercial and industry users in category C at market price, 35 NOK per GPU-hour, so free GPU hours for a company come via the EuroHPC AI Factory calls. Olivia: Cray EX, 448 GPUs, "can be utilised by researchers across Norway". The service centre is funded by EuroHPC JU and FI, CZ, DK, EE, NO, PL.

## Sweden AI Factory (Mimer) {#sweden-ai-factory}

- **Who:** Swedish companies (SMEs and startups first), researchers and the public sector.
- **What:** A publicly funded support service with no supercomputer of its own yet: helps pick the right EuroHPC call (Playground, Fast Lane, Large Scale, AI for Science), reviews the application, offers experts, training and data services.
- **Amount:** No hours of its own; routes you to the EuroHPC calls (Playground "5 000 GPU hours", "up to 3 months"). Own GB200 NVL4 system expected early 2027.
- **Requirements:** None beyond the EuroHPC call you end up in. Large companies may use the compute free but "need to do open science"; SMEs and startups are exempt.
- **How to apply:** Email info@swedenaifactory.se or the contact form at https://swedenaifactory.se/contact-us/; then the founder submits on access.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu and clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://swedenaifactory.se/about/faq/
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15 (root, FAQ and the access-to-compute page; mimer-ai.eu redirects 301 to swedenaifactory.se)
- **Evidence:** "Not yet. As of May 2026, our supercomputer is procured and it is expected to be up and running by early 2027."
- **Gotchas:** "Applications for compute, for all types of organizations from across Europe, are managed through a centralized portal." The access-to-compute page shows hour figures that conflict between versions (AI for Science 360k vs 90 000 GPU hours; Large Scale 22 million vs 30 million) and uses GPU hours where EuroHPC uses node hours; quote the EuroHPC pages instead. "Mimer" appears only in the LinkedIn slug and on the compute page. The Swedish EuroHPC system in the Playground and AI for Science tables is Arrhenius at NAISS.

## NAISS allocations (Small, Medium, Large) {#naiss-allocations}

- **Who:** Researchers affiliated with Swedish academia. Small: "at least a PhD student" with "an appointment of at least 20 percent at a Swedish university or research institution recognised by the Swedish Research Council". Medium: at least an assistant professor or academic research leader with an appointment of at least 60 percent. Large: same plus experience as PI at Medium level and "an established track record of using HPC efficiently".
- **What:** Free compute and storage on NAISS systems (Arrhenius, the new EuroHPC system, replaces all previous NAISS systems from Large Fall 2026) and on the Swedish share of LUMI.
- **Amount:** Not published on the allocation pages; the proposal states the monthly core hour requirement. Large Fall 2026 has "upper limits on the capacity you can request" linked from the round page.
- **Requirements:** Project title, affiliation, English abstract, requested duration and start date, systems, monthly core hour need, Research Council classification. Large also needs the PI's CV (max 2 pages), publication list and project description as PDFs. A PI cannot hold compute in a Medium and a Large project at once.
- **How to apply:** SUPR, https://supr.naiss.se/round/naiss/ (Large Fall 2026: https://supr.naiss.se/round/fall2026large/). Small reviewed weekly (technical, within a week); Medium monthly, submit before the 15th; Large twice a year by NAC. The PI clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://user.naiss.se/allocations/
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15 (naiss.se, user.naiss.se allocations, small, medium and largefall2026 pages)
- **Evidence:** "You must be at least a PhD student and hold an appointment of at least 20 percent at a Swedish university or research institution recognised by the Swedish Research Council"
- **Gotchas:** Large Fall 2026 deadline 16 October 2026 at 15:00 CEST, decisions in November, projects start 1 January 2027 for 6 or 12 months. Alvis and Tetralith are decommissioned, Bianca this autumn, Dardel on 1 January 2027; "It is not possible to request allocations on these systems." LUMI Sweden now accepts CPU-only requests. No GPU hour figures on these pages. Industry is not mentioned anywhere; companies go to the EuroHPC calls.

## Berzelius (NSC, Wallenberg-funded AI cluster) {#berzelius}

- **Who:** "Berzelius is available to eligible Swedish academic research projects connected to a Wallenberg Foundation." Main applicant normally at least an assistant professor (a docent with independent Wallenberg or eligible strategic programme funding may qualify) with active Wallenberg Foundation funding. PhD students and postdocs ask an eligible supervisor to submit.
- **What:** GPU hours on NAISS's specialised AI resource at NSC: Berzelius Ampere (8 A100 per node) and Berzelius Hopper (DGX H200, 8 H200 141 GB per node), shared project storage.
- **Amount:** Default 240 GPU hours per month (small project, fast-track review); maximum monthly request 28 800 GPU hours on Ampere and 11 520 on Hopper; normal maximum project period six months.
- **Requirements:** Wallenberg funding; "The project must not process sensitive data."; requests above the default must be specified and justified.
- **How to apply:** SUPR, Rounds, Local and Special Rounds, KAW, LiU Berzelius 2026. Reviewed monthly except July and December. The PI clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://hpc.pages.naiss.se/user-documentation/berzelius-docs/access/
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15 (NAISS Berzelius docs; the older NSC page nsc.liu.se/support/systems/berzelius-resource-allocation/ agrees on the caps and the KAW affiliation)
- **Evidence:** "Applications requesting the default allocation are considered small projects and receive fast-track review."
- **Gotchas:** The Wallenberg funding gate is not expressible as a matcher rule; ask. Access to one node type does not automatically grant the other; a continuation proposal is preferred over a new one when adding Hopper to an Ampere project. The NSC pages warn that documentation is moving to hpc.pages.naiss.se and older NSC pages may be outdated. No industry access.

## NVIDIA Inception {#nvidia-inception}

- **Who:** Incorporated AI startups less than 10 years old with at least one developer and a working website, at any funding stage. Not consulting or outsourced development firms, cryptocurrency companies, cloud service providers, resellers and distributors, or public companies.
- **What:** Free membership: free cloud credits from NVIDIA and partners, preferred pricing on select NVIDIA hardware and software (a rebate on a subset of enterprise GPUs), free self-paced DLI course codes and workshop discounts, developer forums, VC exposure via Inception Capital Connect.
- **Amount:** Not published on the page.
- **Requirements:** "Your company needs to be incorporated, and you'll need to provide the incorporation date when you apply." Business email at application.
- **How to apply:** https://programs.nvidia.com/phoenix/application with a business email and details about the business and products. The founder clicks submit.
- **Status page:** https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/startups/faq/
- **Verified:** 2026-10-15 (startups page and FAQ; the application page itself is JavaScript-rendered with a captcha and was not read)
- **Evidence:** "Members must employ at least one developer, maintain a working website, be officially incorporated, and be less than 10 years old."
- **Gotchas:** No fees, equity, deadlines or cohorts. "We'll reach out with more information after we review your application", no timeline. The FAQ does not state a work-email-on-own-domain rule; the form embedded on the startups page asks for a "Business Email Address". Cloud credits are described without amounts and do not pay an LLM API invoice.
